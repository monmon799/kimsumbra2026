Introduction

Foundation and Framework of the Internship Program

A. Importance of Internship

An internship serves as a vital bridge between academic theory and professional practice. It allows for the hands-on application of technical skills and the mastery of industry-standard tools. This experience fosters the development of essential soft skills like teamwork, communication, and time management. It also provides valuable exposure to workplace culture and professional networking opportunities. Ultimately, completing an internship builds professional confidence and significantly enhances a candidate's employability in a competitive job market.

B. Objectives of Internship

internship is aimed to bridge the gap between academic theory and practical, professional work, allowing students to gain hands-on experience in their chosen field. It serves as a structured, supervised learning experience designed to prepare interns for the workforce. is seek;

  1. Apply Academic Learning: To take classroom knowledge and apply it to real-world tasks and projects.
  2. Gain Practical Experience: To develop industry-specific skills and learn the daily operations of a professional environment.
  3. Career Exploration: To test your interest in a specific career path or industry before committing to full-time employment.
  4. Develop Professional Skills: To hone both hard skills (technical knowledge) and soft skills (communication, teamwork, problem-solving, and time management).
  5. Network and Build Relationships: To connect with industry experts, mentors, and peers who can guide your career and provide references.
  6. Enhance Employability: To build your resume, making you a more attractive candidate for future, permanent job positions

Company Profile

Overview of the organization, its mission, vision, history, and structure.

A. Nature of Agency

Barangay Caminawit is a local government unit (LGU) and the primary political subdivision of the Municipality of San Jose, Occidental Mindoro. As the basic unit of the Philippine government, the Barangay Hall serves as the administrative center where local policies are implemented and public services are delivered directly to the communit


The Barangay Hall of Caminawit mainly focuses on maintaining public order, promoting community health, and implementing grassroots development projects. Just like other barangays, it is officially recognized under the Local Government Code of 1991 and operates through its executive and legislative arms—the Sangguniang Barangay and the Sangguniang Kabataan. Since its establishment, the barangay has been dedicated to providing its residents with accessible social services and is active in fostering a safe, productive, and self-reliant community.

B. Mission, Vision, Core Values & Goals

  • To gradually solve waste management problems by introducing solid waste management policies throughout the barangay.

C. History / Background

Naming of Caminawit: It is said that the name "Caminawit" originated during the American period, particularly during the operation of the sugar central. The name was derived from the mode of transportation called "kalamazo," an improvised box car running on railroad tracks. People used to wait for the scheduled arrival of the kalamazo coming from the central. American employees would often say "COME & WAIT," and according to local stories, this phrase eventually evolved into the name "CAMINAWIT."

The First Leaders of the Barangay: Formal political organization began in the late 1950s to early 1960s. According to Bergonia Fadri, who was already 16 years old during World War II, Andres Barboza was appointed as Teniente del Barrio, followed by Sergio Gaudiel and Angel Reyes.

The first formal election was conducted in the late 1960s, where Florentino Reyes was elected Teniente del Barrio. He was followed by Francisco Evangelo and Crispin Mariano, who passed away while serving his term. He was succeeded by Dominador Pastrana but was later defeated by Leah Mariano, the widow of Crispin Mariano.

Emmanuel Agustin was elected in 1995 and served until he vacated his position after winning a seat as a Sangguniang Bayan member in 1998. Leonardo Quirante, who was then an incumbent Kagawad, succeeded him as the appointed Barangay Chairman.

In the 2002 elections, Leonardo Quirante was challenged by Danilo A. Centeno, son of Vedasto Centeno from Amaya, Tanza, Cavite, and was defeated. Danilo A. Centeno then served as Barangay Chairman and continued to lead the barangay in the succeeding years.

Economic History (Fishing & Trucking): Surrounded by nearly 70% water, Barangay Caminawit is predominantly a fishing community. The fishing industry dates back to the pre-colonial period and has been sustained by settlers from different parts of the country.

Fish catches from the surrounding waters of San Jose have supported the consumption needs of the entire municipality. The introduction of basnig fishing and later large-scale fishing methods by business groups helped transform Caminawit into a major supplier of fish products to Manila.

In the early 2000s, more advanced fishing operations emerged. "Compressor fishing," although prohibited by law, continued to exist as operators explored deeper seas beyond municipal waters. This also encouraged the growth of trucking businesses, contributing to local employment and trade with Manila.
